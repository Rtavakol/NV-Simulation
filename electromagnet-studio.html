Design question

How much bias is needed to keep eight NV resonance profiles apart while accommodating a test field and temperature drift? Can fixed microwave carriers stay inside the dispersive linear range? What coil can produce that bias?

All eight branches must pass the separation and observability checks. The selected resonance subset is used only for the reconstruction matrix. Coil-axis direction is the fitted bias direction; physically orient the coil accordingly.

Air-core electromagnet and power supply
The coil is centered on the sensor and aligned with the chosen bias. The winding envelope and wire pitch determine the available turns. The design uses all available turns to minimize current within that fixed winding envelope; this does not necessarily minimize heating or cost.

Discrete circular turns are summed using Biot–Savart at the coil centre and two axial sensor endpoints. Bare AWG diameter d = 0.127 × 92^((36−AWG)/39) mm. Copper resistivity at 20 °C = 1.7241×10⁻⁸ Ω·m; R(T) = R20[1 + 0.00393(T−20)]. This is an air-core estimate: winding errors, transverse uniformity, cooling, inductance, thermal rise and magnetic materials are not modeled. Copper temperature is an input, not a predicted equilibrium. The current-density limit is a user assumption, not a wire ampacity certification.


Methods, limitations and references
The field search is limited to 0.1–30 mT to avoid the aligned ground-state anticrossing region. It screens an electronic S=1 model, refines the six best sampled directions with the full S=1, I=1 model, probes larger fields when the separation minimum fails the linear tests, and samples temperature endpoints/midpoint and 27 box points (or ellipsoid directions). Reported candidates are numerical designs on the chosen search grid, not a proof of the global minimum or of continuous-domain coverage. Re-run with more directions and a smaller field step for convergence.
All four NV orientations are included using isotropically averaged microwave polarization for design. Each profile is represented by the simulator’s central transition and nearby observable hyperfine transitions. A nonzero laboratory test-field offset is not imported; the domain is centred on zero test field. The Toyli D(T) model and current Hamiltonian constants are used; below 27 °C this is an extrapolation. No strain, power broadening, spin dynamics or coil heating feedback is included.
The linear half-range is the contiguous region around the central transition where the isolated-profile sinusoidal first-harmonic FM response has a local slope within the chosen ±fractional tolerance of its slope at the working point. Three-tone mode sums equal-power tones separated by |A∥|; no saturation or additional broadening is modeled. A measured half-range overrides this estimate. Ideal temperature feed-forward subtracts each branch’s calculated zero-test-field thermal shift; it assumes exact temperature knowledge and carrier control.
The frequency allocation includes the drift reserve, each branch’s hyperfine half-span, FM excursion, extra tone offsets and the linewidth guard. This deliberately keeps the swept allocations disjoint; it is more conservative than checking only instantaneous line spacings. Increasing bias cannot by itself fix insufficient dispersive capture range.
