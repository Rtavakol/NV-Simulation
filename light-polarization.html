Optical model and limitations

Each NV has two orthogonal optical dipoles perpendicular to its axis n. For linear light, excitation is proportional to 1−(E·n)². For circular or unpolarized transverse light, it is [1+(k·n)²]/2. A polarizer obeys Malus’ law for a linear source and transmits half of ideal unpolarized or circular light. A half-wave plate rotates linear polarization by twice the plate angle relative to its input axis without Malus attenuation.

For equal, incoherently populated emission dipoles in the narrow collection-direction approximation, collection without an analyzer is [1+(u·n)²]/2; with a transverse linear analyzer a, it is [1−(a·n)²]/2. Excitation and collection factors multiply each orientation’s illustrative spin-dependent PL spectrum. The brightness reference is unit incident power, isotropic excitation (2/3 per NV), all four equal NV populations and no analyzer; it is not an absolute photon-rate prediction.

Propagation, polarization and analyzer vectors are transverse by construction. This model omits refraction, Fresnel losses, total internal reflection, finite numerical aperture, saturation, orbital-selective pumping, polarization memory and optical spin-pumping dynamics. Circular and unpolarized excitation give identical orientation weights here. Microwave B₁ is independent of optical E.

Reference: Magnetic sensitivity enhancement via polarimetric excitation and detection of an ensemble of NV centers (2024). The shared Hamiltonian uses the simulator’s ¹⁴N model and temperature shift.
