Reconstruct the bias field from measured resonances

Enter the eight electronic-branch centre frequencies in MHz, ordered from low to high. Use the central hyperfine line of each triplet, rather than all 24 hyperfine lines. Set the diamond orientation, temperature, D at 25 °C and hyperfine parameters above to match your experiment.

Ready · fill eight frequencies, then reconstruct.

Direction ambiguity: eight unlabelled frequencies do not generally determine a unique field direction. Reversing B and permuting the equivalent crystal axes can reproduce the same spectrum. The tool lists compatible symmetry-related solutions and initially chooses the one nearest the current simulated bias; this reference is a preference, not extra measured evidence. With nonzero test-field offsets, the compatible solutions can also imply different bias magnitudes.

Tracked resonance frequencies

Resonances 1–8 are ordered by frequency at t = 0. Each is represented by a central hyperfine transition of one electronic branch. Labels stay with their branches during the run; exact degeneracies and strongly mixed or dark branches can make the assignment ambiguous.

Select any individual resonance or combination, including pairs. Display selection can change during or after a run; CSV and PNG use the selected traces.

NV1NV2NV3NV4

Constant uses its value. Sinusoidal/square/triangle waves span minimum to maximum. Damped oscillations decay toward their midpoint with envelope exp(−t/τ). Noise is bounded uniform sample-and-hold, updated at the selected frequency, with a reproducible seed. Step changes from minimum to maximum at the step time; drift ramps from minimum to maximum across the full duration. Frequency and phase apply to periodic functions; decay applies to damped and step time to step. Maximum 10,001 samples; periodic and noise inputs require at least 10 samples per cycle/update interval.

Frequency branches are followed by maximum eigenstate overlap, rather than re-sorting peaks at every step. At exact degeneracies identities can be ambiguous. A tracked frequency can remain visible here even when its PL weight becomes negligible; CSV includes the instantaneous weight. Dynamics are quasi-static: no spin relaxation, detector bandwidth, thermal lag or field-coil response is simulated. Playback may slow on busy devices; calculated samples are never skipped.

Physics, conventions & limits

H/h = D S_z² + γe B·S − γn B·I + A∥ S_z I_z + A⊥ (S_x I_x + S_y I_y) + Q I_z². S = 1 and I = 1 (¹⁴N), with γe = 28.025 MHz/mT. Default constants: D = 2870 MHz, A∥ = −2.16 MHz, A⊥ = −2.70 MHz, Q = −4.95 MHz and γn = +3.077 MHz/T; the nuclear Zeeman term uses a minus sign. Each NV uses its own local frame. Its local x is chosen along the transverse static field, making H real without changing the physics; microwave polarization is transformed into the same frame. All eigenstate pairs are considered, including above the aligned ground-state crossing near 102.4 mT.

Crystal axes: [111], [1 −1 −1], [−1 1 −1], [−1 −1 1], normalized in crystal coordinates and mapped into the selected surface frame, then rotated into the lab. For (001), surface-frame x ∥ [100]; for (110) and (111), x ∥ [1 −1 0]. Surface-frame y = normal × x. The crystal cube and all NV axes rotate together; the magnetic field remains in lab coordinates. θ is measured from +z; φ from +x toward +y. Rotate view changes only the camera: lab axes, diamond and field move together on screen, while field components and resonance frequencies stay fixed. Steer B changes the physical field. The cube follows crystal [100], [010], and [001] edges; the four NV axes follow its body diagonals. Selecting a cut aligns that crystallographic normal with lab z before the lab rotations are applied.

PL is drawn as Lorentzian dips weighted by microwave coupling × (P₀,i − P₀,j)², with P₀ the electron mₛ = 0 probability in an eigenstate. A factor of 2/3 (including equal weighting of the three nuclear states) makes an isolated low-field, transverse linearly driven triplet sum to the contrast-scale setting. This phenomenological model assumes equal nuclear weighting and does not solve optical pumping, relaxation, nuclear polarization, excited-state anticrossings or saturation. At strong transverse fields and near crossings, actual fluorescence can differ greatly. Temperature changes D using P(TK) = 0.097 TK − 0.00037 TK² + 0.00000017 TK³ MHz, with D(T) = D(25 °C) + P(T + 273.15) − P(298.15). The Toyli cubic was fitted around 300–700 K; the 0–27 °C part is a short extrapolation, and the absolute offset is set by your D at 25 °C. Temperature dependence of hyperfine parameters, linewidth, optical brightness and relaxation is omitted. Test fields are added in lab coordinates in µT and converted to mT before solving the Hamiltonian. Strain, anisotropic g and noise are omitted. The gold D(T) line and grey D(25 °C) line are references, not generally resonances at nonzero field. At 1 T aligned with an NV, the main electronic transitions are near 25.155 and 30.895 GHz, split by hyperfine coupling.

Reference: Felton et al., Hyperfine interaction in the ground state of the negatively charged nitrogen vacancy center in diamond, Physical Review B 79, 075203 (2009). Parameters here are representative rounded values and can be edited above.

Temperature reference: Toyli et al., Measurement and Control of Single Nitrogen-Vacancy Center Spins above 600 K (2012).
