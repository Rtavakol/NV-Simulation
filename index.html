Physics, conventions & limits

Electronic frequencies are obtained by diagonalizing H/h = D S²z + γe B·S independently for each NV axis, using D = 2870 MHz and γe = 28.025 MHz/mT. Axial symmetry allows the transverse field to be placed along the local x axis without changing the eigenvalues. Frequencies are the two gaps from the lowest eigenstate over this 0–20 mT range.

Crystal axes: [111], [1 −1 −1], [−1 1 −1], [−1 −1 1], normalized in crystal coordinates and mapped into the selected surface frame, then rotated into the lab. For (001), surface-frame x ∥ [100]; for (110) and (111), x ∥ [1 −1 0]. Surface-frame y = normal × x. The crystal cube and all NV axes rotate together; the magnetic field remains in lab coordinates. θ is measured from +z; φ from +x toward +y. The cube follows crystal [100], [010], and [001] edges; the four NV axes follow its body diagonals. Selecting a cut aligns that crystallographic normal with lab z before the lab rotations are applied.

Hyperfine structure uses fixed, equal-intensity ¹⁴N triplets, rather than a full electron–nuclear Hamiltonian. Near zero field, overlapping resonances, or strong transverse mixing, this approximation does not reproduce hyperfine mixing or nuclear selection rules. Equal electronic contrast is illustrative: microwave polarization, optical spin mixing, strain, temperature, power broadening and noise are omitted. Contrast specifies the summed amplitude of each triplet; its central dip depth depends on linewidth and overlap. This is an educational spectrum, not a sensitivity prediction.

Reference: Local and bulk ¹³C hyperpolarization in NV-centred diamonds, Nature Communications (2015). The reference describes the electronic Hamiltonian and the approximately 2.16 MHz ¹⁴N splitting.
